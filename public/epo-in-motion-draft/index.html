08 / Why dance

Physical. Human.
Unexpected.
Joyful. Alive.

An anniversary film about patents could easily turn retrospective and institutional. We want the opposite. Dance needs no translation. It brings energy, humour and emotion, and gives employees a reason to take part instead of just appearing.

The making-of becomes part of the idea

09 / Movement language

We don’t teach the EPO a dance.
We turn its work into movement.

A hand passes a document. Two people compare. A group gathers around an idea. With a leading choreographer, these real gestures become a movement language that belongs to the EPO and nobody else.

Small gestures grow into larger movements. Movements become patterns, and patterns become systems. In the end, hundreds of individuals create something only collaboration can achieve.

Route 1 / The relay

One movement. Five cities.
It never stops.

A single gesture begins at a desk in Munich. It is passed from colleague to colleague and from city to city, grows with every handover, and returns to where it started.

The handover cut

Every movement leaves the frame and continues in the next shot, in the next city. The film never stops moving.

Always forward

The camera always travels left to right. Five shooting days read as one continuous flow.

35 soloists, 35 nationalities

Each short scene features a different colleague, captioned with first name and country.

One group scene per city

20 to 30 people per location. All other shots use one to four people.

Route 2 / The rehearsal

It starts as a video call.
It ends as a music video.

Five colleagues meet for their Wednesday call. Only Peter in Brussels remembers that today is the choreography rehearsal for the anniversary shoot. What follows is clumsy, funny and completely contagious.

Five colleagues, five cities

Sylvia in Munich, Robert in The Hague, Ariane in Berlin, Betty in Vienna and Peter in Brussels.

From video call to music video

It starts as a split screen. The camera language slowly opens up into a cinematic, moving film.

Comedy first

An unmuted microphone, an endless typing indicator and a shoot everyone forgot.

Mistakes welcome

It is only rehearsal day. Talented or hopeless, everyone can join in.

Route 2 / Four scenes

From muted to moving.

SCENE 01

0:00 to 1:00

An ordinary morning

The Wednesday call across five cities. Peter unmutes, Happy Birthday blasts through everyone’s headphones, and the truth comes out: the rehearsal is today.

SCENE 02

1:00 to 1:30

The rehearsal

Peter turns his laptop round and demonstrates. One by one the others follow, hesitant, wrong direction, pretending to stretch. Then they find their groove.

SCENE 03

1:30 to 2:30

It’s contagious

Colleagues stop in the corridors. Sylvia pulls out her headphones and the music fills the floor. The dance spreads through all five cities until they blend into one.

SCENE 04

2:30 to 3:00

Back to reality

Out of breath, back in the call. “Same time tomorrow?” Tomorrow is the actual shoot. Ariane slowly reaches for the mute button.

Film length about 3 minutes. The final title: 50 years of moving ideas forward. Together.

14 / Roadmap

Four ways to make it. One we recommend.

Every option is built on the real EPO locations. AI can support individual shots in any of them; the heart of the film stays human.

OPTION 01

Choreographer + employees

EPO employees learn and perform the choreography on location. The most authentic option: the rehearsals become an internal event.

Staff involvement

High

OPTION 02

Choreographer + professional dancers

Professional dancers perform at the real EPO locations. Maximum precision and visual impact; staff watch rather than join.

Staff involvement

Low

OPTION 03

Recommended

Choreographer + employees + selected performers

Real EPO employees carry the film. Selected professional performers take on the demanding passages. Authentic and precise.

Staff involvement

High

OPTION 04

Full AI video

The film is generated with AI, built on the real EPO locations. Fast and flexible, but no one takes part.

Staff involvement

None
